# Format: curiosity — "What if…": an absurd premise, its chain of consequences, a real fact, an ironic loop

Owner's model (2026-11-13): *"O governo começou a pagar R$ 1 toda vez que alguém…"* → nobody goes to work →
bread costs R$ 300, then R$ 1 million → the real lesson (Germany 1923, people burning banknotes for heat) →
the richest man is a 90-year-old farmer with 10,000 hens; people pay in eggs → the old boss begs him for a job
and gets his own line back. Not horror: a funny, clever thought experiment that teaches one real thing.
Follow the 5-step formula in `STORY_BRIEF.md`.

## Shape (third person / "you", present or past, 61–80 s)
1. **Absurd premise as a fact (0–3 s)**, one line, with a precise number: *"The government started paying
   everyone €1 for every step they took."* Then *"Sounds fake, right? But that's exactly what happened."*
2. **The mechanic (by 10 s):** how it works, concretely (a wristband counts every step, the money lands at
   once). One sceptic says it won't change anything — **remember his line, it comes back at the end.**
3. **Chain of consequences, each bigger and each with a number** (Monday nobody at work → buses stop → one guy
   makes €40,000 before lunch → bread costs €300 → by Friday €1 million → people push trolleys of cash for
   chewing gum). A new consequence every 5–7 s.
4. **The real-world fact (the "curiosity"):** one sentence that explains the mechanism plainly + one true
   historical or scientific example that proves it (*"when money is created without producing anything, it
   loses value — Germany, 1923"*). Check it with a web search before writing; if unsure, leave it out.
5. **Ironic twist + loop:** who wins is the one person who ignored the madness (the farmer with hens), and the
   sceptic's line comes back reversed as the last line. Then a one-word comment question
   (*"Would you have quit your job? Yes or no."*).

## Rules
- TikTok-safe and brand-safe: the premise may be absurd, never sexual, political, or about real people,
  parties, brands or apps. No financial advice. Keep it light.
- Numbers drive the story: show each one big on screen as it is said (prices, counts, years).
- Read the log: never repeat a premise or the same real-world lesson (inflation, supply and demand, the
  tragedy of the commons, the cobra effect, Goodhart's law, opportunity cost, diminishing returns…).

## Title / caption
Title = the premise in 2–4 words (*€1 Per Step*). Caption = the question + 3–5 hashtags, e.g.
*"Would you quit your job for this? #curiosidades #whatif #economia #historia #animation"*.
