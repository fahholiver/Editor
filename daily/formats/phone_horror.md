# Format: phone_horror — the story happens on a phone screen

Most beats are shown as the engine's phone UI (`phone_frame`, `call_ui`, message bubbles as in
production/render.py `s_messages`), cut with short shots of the person reading it in a dark room.

## Shape
1. **Hook = the first message**, quoted, alone: *“Are you awake? Don't turn the light on.”* — then who sent
   it and the time (*From: Mum. 2:51 a.m.*).
2. **Exchange** (4–8 beats): the character replies; each answer from the other side is a little too
   precise (knows what the character is wearing, where they are standing, what is behind them).
3. **The check** — the character calls the number, or looks at the contact, last seen, a photo the other
   side sends. Call screens and "typing…" bubbles are the suspense.
4. **The tell** — a plain fact that makes the messages impossible (*Mum's phone was in the drawer
   beside him. He'd charged it for her.*).
5. **Sting** — a final message or notification that arrives right now (*New photo: taken from inside his
   wardrobe.*).

## Voice
Narrator describes, and reads the messages in curly quotes (the captions tint quotes cream). Show
sender names and times as concrete text so the animator can put them on screen. Third person with a
name (Leah, Sam, Josh…) or second person.

## Title / caption
Title = the first message or the sender (*Don't Turn the Light On*, *Message From Mum*).
