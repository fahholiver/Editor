# Format: rule_horror — "If X, do Y" horror (the Check the Radiator / deer-and-porch-light style)

Follow the 5-step formula in `STORY_BRIEF.md`. Model: *"In our town, there's one rule. If a deer is standing in
your yard at night, never turn on the porch light."*

## Shape
1. **Hook = the rule, then the rule broken (0–5 s):** line 1 states the rule alone; line 2 breaks it at once
   (*"Last night, my little brother flipped the switch."*). Or a rule with an absurd mechanic
   (*"Your cat grows to the size of whatever he's hunting. Tonight, he's seven feet tall."*).
2. **Grounding** (2–3 short lines): who, where, and **a precise time that will come back** (*9:41*). One wrong
   detail that breaks nature (*"It didn't run. Deer always run."*).
3. **A second voice adds the hidden rule** — a neighbour's text, a call, a note (*"It'll come back every night,
   one step closer each time."*). Now the viewer knows the countdown.
4. **Escalation by repetition** — the same time, the same place, one step closer each beat (night 1: edge of
   the lawn · night 2: halfway · night 3: the porch steps). Each beat is a visible position or a count.
5. **The tell / the trap** — one plain sentence that flips the rule (*"That's not your switch."*,
   *"Emma was an only child."*). The rule they trusted was the danger.
6. **Sting + CTA** — the last threat is closer than ever and echoes the hook object (the light, the number).
   Then one line for the comments: *"Would you have turned the light off? Yes or no."* — or
   *"Follow for part two."* if it really is a part one.

## Voice
First person plural/singular from inside a family or town, or a named third person (Daniel, Emma, Adam…).
Calm, matter-of-fact narration; the calmer the voice, the scarier the rule.

## On screen
The rule as a big tag when first said and again when broken; the recurring time as a clock card each night.

## Title / caption
Title = the rule's action (*Check the Radiator*, *Never Turn It On*). Caption = the rule + the comment question.
