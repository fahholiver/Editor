# Baltimore Key Bridge pilot

Working title **Why Baltimore’s Key Bridge Collapsed**

The March 26, 2024 case centers on the container ship *Dali*. The NTSB identified a loose signal wire connection stemming from improper installation of wire-label banding. Its final report traces the blackout, loss of propulsion and steering near the bridge, and the bridge's vulnerability to vessel impact. Open the report before writing. Attribute findings to the NTSB and give the pier protection and vulnerability findings space in the explanation. [NTSB case page](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/pages/DCA24MM031.aspx) · [Final report MIR-25-40](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/MIR2540.pdf)

## Rough ten-minute running order

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | Label the salvage or response footage and open on the question of how electrical trouble on a ship led to a bridge collapse. |
| 0:25–1:40 | Establish the approach, the ship, and the bridge span using a labeled schematic and scene still. |
| 1:40–3:30 | Explain normal ship power, the signal connection and what changed. Keep earlier and later blackouts distinct. |
| 3:30–5:45 | Reconstruct the approach and loss of propulsion and steering. Label timing and animations using the final report. |
| 5:45–7:30 | Animate the strike and span failure. Use a simple structural diagram and explain pier vulnerability. |
| 7:30–9:30 | Walk through investigator findings and recommendations. Distinguish proposed changes from completed changes. |
| 9:30–10:00 | Close on the safeguards that could interrupt this chain of failure. |

For diagrams, use a generic terminal block, an overhead ship approach, and a bridge elevation. Label simplified layouts as schematics. Synchronize all shot and diagram cues after the real voiceover is available.
