# Finding topics, and where the numbers come from

## Topics people want
1. Search YouTube (web search, or the user's browser if connected) for "rise and fall of", "how X went bankrupt", "what happened to", "how X makes money", "the collapse of", "the downfall of", "biggest fraud", "the economics of". Note videos from the last 12 months that beat their channel's size (20K subscribers, 500K+ views), with subject, length and title shape.
2. Strong topics: a famous brand people grew up with, one clear mistake, real numbers that exist, and a twist (the challenger, the fraud, the comeback).
3. For each idea give: the title, the hook moment, the demand proof you actually saw (title, channel size, views, age), whether numbers and free photos exist, sensitivity (low / medium / skip), suggested length.
Never make up view counts, trends or search volumes. If you couldn't check, say "unverified".

## Where real numbers come from (all free)
- **SEC EDGAR** (sec.gov/edgar): annual reports (10-K) of US-listed companies. "Selected Financial Data" gives 5 years of revenue and profit; "Properties" or "Store Development" gives store counts. Read them with web fetch.
- **Annual reports** on the company's investor-relations site, and press releases.
- **Court records and SEC enforcement releases** for frauds.
- **Reputable news** (Reuters, AP, CNBC, Bloomberg, the Wall Street Journal, the New York Times) for events and quotes, cited by name.
- **Wikipedia** only to find the primary source, then cite that source.
Restated figures: if two filings give different numbers for the same year, use the original and note the restatement in `sources.md`.
